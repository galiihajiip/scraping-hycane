# 10 Academic and Expert Evidence

Keep scientific/expert evidence separate from customer voice.

## Discovery
Prefer OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, publisher pages, university repositories, SINTA-indexed sources where publicly verifiable, and Scopus metadata when legitimately accessible.

## Search domains
Hydroponic adoption; urban/home agriculture; beginner barriers; pH/EC/TDS/nutrients/water/temperature/oxygen; smart hydroponics; IoT; automation; AI; prediction; growing media; bagasse; biodegradable materials; consumer behavior; technology acceptance; sustainability; digital marketing.

## Source quality
Strong: peer-reviewed, systematic review, official institutional guidance, identifiable expert with relevant credentials. Weak: anonymous posts, sales pages dressed as expert evidence, content farms.

## Extraction
For each source record: claim, method, context/sample, finding, limitation, relevance to HYCANE, evidence strength, URL, DOI, retrieval date.

## Triangulation
If social + academic + expert agree, confidence is stronger. If only social evidence exists, call it customer signal. If technical literature says a problem matters but customers rarely mention it, flag possible low salience. Record contradictions rather than smoothing them away.

## Output
Create `outputs/academic_expert_synthesis.md` with high-confidence findings, unresolved questions, product implications, segmentation implications, and proposal implications.
